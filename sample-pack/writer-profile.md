# About me — Maya Reed

*(Draft sample-pack file for the course's fictional writer. Written in her own voice, the way someone would jot notes about themselves for a new assistant.)*

## What I do

I'm a freelance writer. I publish on Medium about productivity and working with AI tools (lately a lot about AI agents, explained for people who aren't engineers), mostly for people who have a full-time job and are trying to get more done without burning out. Think busy team leads, early-career founders, people who've tried every to-do app and given up on all of them.

I've been on Medium for about four years. I'm in one publication, Slow Systems (an editor, Dan, reviews my pieces before they go out there), and I post the rest on my own profile. I also do 1–2 paid ghostwriting pieces a month for a small SaaS company's blog, but those are confidential.

People tell me I'm the one who "actually tries the thing" before writing about it. I don't do listicles of 27 tools. I pick one idea, test it on my own week, and write what happened, including what didn't work.

## My week

- **Monday:** I go through my idea list in Notion and pick what I'll write this week. Usually takes an hour because I second-guess everything.
- **Tuesday and Friday:** publishing days. I aim for two articles a week, about 1,000–1,500 words each. Honestly it's more like three every two weeks.
- **Writing a piece:** I start with a rough outline in Notion, write a messy first draft in Google Docs, leave it overnight, then edit hard. The edit takes longer than the draft.
- **Diagrams:** most of my AI articles have one. I draw them in Excalidraw and export PNGs. Rounded boxes and arrows, the handwriting font, soft pastel colours where each colour means something.
- **Feedback:** Dan emails me his notes (Gmail). I keep the useful ones in a "Dan notes" label.
- **Ideas:** I record voice memos when I'm walking and dump the transcripts into a Google Drive folder. Most of them I never look at again, which bugs me.

## What makes a piece good (to me)

- It opens with a real moment from my own week, not a statistic and never a question.
- One idea per article. If I need "and also", it's two articles.
- Short paragraphs, but not one-sentence-paragraph LinkedIn style.
- I always say what didn't work. Readers trust that.
- The headline promises something specific. "How I…" or a number of days/weeks works for me. No "Ultimate guide".
- No "in today's fast-paced world", no "game-changer", no "let's dive in". Dan cuts these instantly.

## What I'd love help with

Picking ideas on Monday, and the first edit pass. A first draft from my notes would help too: I lose the first half hour of every draft staring at a blank page. As long as every moment and number in it is mine. Also, turning those voice memos into something useful. I'd also love the diagrams to be quicker: I spend 30–40 minutes on each one.

## Apps

Notion (content calendar + outlines), Google Docs/Drive (drafts, voice memo transcripts), Gmail (Dan's feedback), Medium (publishing), Excalidraw (diagrams).
