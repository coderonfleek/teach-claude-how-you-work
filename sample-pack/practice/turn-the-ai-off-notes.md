# When to turn the AI off: my notes

*(Exported from my Notion outline page, 2 Nov 2026. Rough. Everything here really happened, and the times are from my timer app.)*

## The moment
- Tuesday 6 October, 8:40 a.m., kitchen table, Biscuit asleep on my feet, second coffee going cold.
- The planner AI (the one from my September piece) had put "write the cost piece" at 4:30 p.m., right after I told it nothing that needs thinking after 4. It also booked my call with Dan twice, on Tuesday and Thursday, and gave "walk Biscuit" a 15-minute slot at 1 p.m., which is when Biscuit is asleep.
- I spent **20 minutes** fixing its plan. Writing a plan myself takes **6 minutes** (timed on paper the following Monday).
- My partner, walking past with toast: "Is it planning your day or are you planning its day?"

## What the planner did
- Every morning at 7:30 it read my calendar and my task list in Notion and wrote a plan for the day: time slots for each task, calls, breaks.
- I'd read it at the kitchen table after the walk, then fix it. The fixing is what I timed.
- Planning was really three jobs: putting tasks into time slots, booking calls around other people, and spotting clashes. It was bad at the first two and good at the third.

## The numbers
- AI week, Mon 5 – Fri 9 Oct: **1 hour 22 minutes** in total fixing its plans. Mon 18, Tue 20, Wed 17, Thu 16, Fri 11 minutes.
- Paper, Mon 12 – Wed 14 Oct: an A5 notebook. Planning took **6, 5 and 7 minutes**. 18 minutes for three days.
- Even its best day (11) took longer than my worst day on paper (7).
- Since Mon 19 Oct: I plan on paper every morning (about 6 minutes), and the AI does one job only, checking my week for clashes. Fixing its work takes about **2 minutes** a day. It caught two clashes in the first week.

## The idea (one idea)
If correcting the AI's work takes longer than doing it yourself, turn it off for that job. Not off forever, off for that job.
**Careful:** this is almost a slogan. Put it in the middle, never at the end.

## Everyday comparison
Biscuit's long lead. It's meant to give him more freedom, but if I spend the whole walk untangling it from lamp posts, the short lead gets us home quicker. I still use the long lead in the park, where there's nothing to wrap it round.

## What didn't work
- **Paper forgot things.** I didn't copy unfinished tasks to the next day. Renewing Biscuit's insurance vanished on Wednesday, and I only noticed the following Monday.
- **I missed the one thing the AI was good at.** On paper I booked two calls back to back with no break on Wednesday. The AI always spotted that. I ate lunch standing up.
- **My fault, not the AI's:** I'd told it "keep afternoons light". That means nothing. It couldn't follow an instruction I couldn't have explained myself. When I rewrote it as "no writing tasks after 4 p.m., calls only", it still got Tuesday wrong, but less often.

## Try this week
Pick one day. Time how long you spend fixing your AI's plan, then plan the next day yourself and time that. Compare the two numbers.
