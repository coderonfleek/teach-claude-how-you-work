## Lesson 1, Step 4 — What's wrong with the "before" article

**What would you change first?**
The opening. "You know how it goes" is so generic. I'd open with something real, like the morning I opened my Notion and found 400 unread clippings. Never a hypothetical "you".

**Is the headline something you'd write?**
No. It's clever but it doesn't promise anything. I'd write something like "I abandoned 3 second brains in 2 years. Here's what finally stuck."

**Which sentences sound least like you?**
"You're not alone, and you're not lazy." Sounds like a self-help Instagram post. And the numbered list of 5 reasons, I'd never do that, it's one idea per article for me.

**What's missing that you'd always include?**
What didn't work for me. I always say what I tried that failed. And a real number from my own week.

**Would you use that diagram? What would your version look like?**
The idea is ok but I'd draw it in Excalidraw: rounded boxes and arrows, the handwriting font, pastel colours that each mean something. Short labels, two or three words.

**If you had to keep one idea from the piece, which one?**
Building the system before you have a reason. That's the one I actually lived.

**How would you describe your tone? Who are you picturing?**
Honest, a bit dry, like a friend who tried the thing so you don't have to. I picture a team lead reading on their phone on the train.

**How do you usually end an article?**
I end with one small thing to try this week. Not a slogan.

**Words or habits that make you cringe?**
"Game-changer", "let's dive in", "in today's fast-paced world", starting with a question, and one-sentence paragraphs stacked up like LinkedIn.

## Lesson 1, Step 5 — Work-map interview (gaps the profile doesn't cover)

**What can Claude do in each app?**
Read only for Gmail. Notion: read, create new outlines, and add new ideas to my content calendar, but never edit or move existing pages. Google Docs/Drive: read and create new drafts, never edit or overwrite existing files. Medium: you can create drafts there but never publish. Excalidraw: I'd just want the diagram images.

**What must Claude never do?**
Never publish on Medium. Never send or reply to emails, including to Dan. Never read or use the confidential ghostwriting work. Never mention the SaaS company or my ghostwriting in my articles. Never make up a "real moment" or a number from my week. Never delete or overwrite my drafts.

**What should Claude take on first?**
1. Picking ideas on Monday (that's where I lose the most time). 2. The first edit pass. 3. A first draft from my notes, so I'm editing instead of staring at a blank page, but every moment and number has to be mine. 4. Making diagrams quicker. 5. Turning voice memos into something useful.

**Where does your older work live?**
Medium is the true copy. Final drafts are in Google Docs. Diagram PNGs are in a Drive folder called "Diagrams".

**How long does each step take?**
Outline 30 minutes, first draft 2 hours, edit 2 hours plus.

**What do you always catch or fix when you edit?**
An intro that takes too long, vague claims with no real example or number, and a headline that doesn't match what the piece delivers. Also, I cut the first paragraph almost every time because it's throat-clearing.

**How do you tell a good idea from a weak one?**
I've actually tried it or can try it this week. It's one idea, no "and also". A team lead would care about it on the train. I haven't written something too close to it recently.

**Should the ghostwriting be in the work map?**
Only mention that it exists (1–2 pieces a month, confidential, hands off). No details.

**Anything to run on a schedule?**
A Sunday evening shortlist of 3–5 ideas for Monday, from Notion and that week's voice memos. And whenever Dan emails, a short summary of his notes plus a running list of what he keeps flagging. A monthly look-back at what I published: maybe later, mark it OPEN.
